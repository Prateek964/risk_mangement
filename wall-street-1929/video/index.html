The Wall Street Crash
1929
October 1929.
Wall Street was about to
experience one of the most
devastating financial collapses
in history.
But this disaster didn't begin
with panic.
It began with greed,
easy money,
and the belief that stock
prices could only go up.
Throughout the 1920s,
America experienced an economic
boom.
Businesses expanded,
consumer spending increased,
and millions of investors rushed
into the stock market.
Many bought shares using borrowed
money, a practice known as
buying on margin.
As prices climbed,
rising prices attracted more
buyers.
And more buyers pushed prices
even higher.
The market began to look
unstoppable.
But beneath the optimism,
danger was building.
Stock prices had become detached
from what many companies could
realistically earn.
Speculation was rampant,
and borrowed money magnified
the risks.
Eventually, confidence began
to weaken.
Investors started selling.
And suddenly...
everyone wanted to get out
at once.
On October 24,
1929, known as Black Thursday,
panic selling shook Wall Street.
Five days later,
on Black Tuesday,
October 29,
selling intensified dramatically.
Investors rushed to dump their
shares.
Prices collapsed.
Fortunes disappeared,
sometimes within hours.
Those who had borrowed to
invest faced devastating losses,
and some were forced to
sell simply to repay their
debts.
The market's upward spiral
had turned into a downward
avalanche.
But the crash was not
the entire Great Depression.
Bank failures,
collapsing demand,
falling production,
and policy mistakes helped
turn the stock market disaster
into a prolonged economic catastrophe.
Businesses shut down.
Unemployment soared.
Families lost savings,
homes, and livelihoods.
The crisis spread far beyond
Wall Street,
affecting economies around
the world.
The Wall Street Crash revealed
a timeless truth about financial
markets.
When speculation replaces fundamentals,
debt magnifies risk,
and confidence turns into panic,
a boom can become a
disaster.
Because markets don't just
run on money.
They run on trust.
And once that trust disappears,
the consequences can last for
years.
