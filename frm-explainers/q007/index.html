PRACTICE QUESTION
THE QUESTION

A junior analyst has just started working for a national banking supervisor and is training for a position as a bank examiner. As part of the training program, the analyst is asked to explain how banking regulations evolved as a result of the 2007 – 2009 financial crisis to encourage better risk governance. Which of the following correctly describes an impact of regulations that were introduced as a result of the crisis?

Which post-crisis regulatory impact is correct?
❚❚ Pause & pick your answer 321
A✓✕
Banks were required to securitize all the mortgages they originate in order to distribute risk across financial institutions.
B✓✕
Banks were encouraged to establish an independent risk management function with access to the board of directors.
C✓✕
Proprietary trading operations were merged with traditional banking operations to provide banks better governance over their trading desks.
D✓✕
Derivatives were encouraged to be traded OTC rather than centrally cleared to provide greater transparency.
CORRECT ANSWER
B✓✕
Banks were encouraged to establish an independent risk management function with access to the board of directors.
Independent risk, direct line to the board
THE PROBLEM
Risk reported to the business it watched
THE FIX
Independent risk function
Direct line to the board
Board hears the risks
WHY NOT THE OTHERS?
A
Banks were required to securitize all the mortgages they originate in order to distribute risk across financial institutions.
Securitization helped cause the crisis — it wasn’t encouraged.
C
Proprietary trading operations were merged with traditional banking operations to provide banks better governance over their trading desks.
Opposite — the Volcker rule banned proprietary trading at banks.
D
Derivatives were encouraged to be traded OTC rather than centrally cleared to provide greater transparency.
Regulators pushed central clearing, not more OTC trading.
KEY TAKEAWAY
1Make risk management independent
2Give it a direct line to the board
3Less prop trading, more central clearing

Okay, pause the video and lock

And the answer is...
B!
Here's why.
A key lesson from the crisis
was that risk managers got overruled.
When the risk function reports
to the trading desk or the
business it's supposed to watch,
it can be silenced.
So regulators pushed for an independent
risk function, with a direct line
to the board.
That way, the board actually hears
about risk issues.
Now, why not the others?
A is wrong.
Securitization was actually one
of the causes of the crisis.
Highly rated mortgage tranches
collapsed together, so regulators
didn't push for more of it.
C is wrong too.
It was the opposite.
Rules like the Volcker rule banned
banks from proprietary trading
and pushed it out of banking.
And D?
Also wrong.
Post-crisis rules encouraged central
clearing of derivatives wherever
possible, not more OTC trading.
So, the key takeaway.
After the crisis,
banks were pushed to make risk
management independent and give
it a direct line to the
board.
At the same time,
rules limited proprietary trading
and moved derivatives toward central
clearing.
