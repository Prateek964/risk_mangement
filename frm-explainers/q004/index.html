THE QUESTION

The newly hired CFO of a publicly traded computer manufacturing company is assessing the concerns and motivations of different stakeholder groups. The CFO focuses on the perspectives of these stakeholders on the firm’s hedging strategies. Which of the following statements is correct?

Which statement about stakeholders and hedging is correct?
❚❚ Pause & pick your answer 321
A✓✕
If the firm’s equity investors hold a well-diversified portfolio, they would typically prefer that the firm hedge risks specific to the computer industry.
B✓✕
Debt investors would typically prefer that the company use hedging strategies to increase the stability of its revenue stream.
C✓✕
Both equity and debt investors would typically prefer that the firm not hedge the foreign exchange risk of long-term contracts with international customers.
D✓✕
Equity investors would typically not reward the firm for using hedging to reduce its tax exposure over a multi-year period.
CORRECT ANSWER
B✓✕
Debt investors would typically prefer that the company use hedging strategies to increase the stability of its revenue stream.
Lenders want stability, not upside
DEBT INVESTORS GET
Fixed interest + principal back
SO THEY WANT
Little upside
Real downside
Stable revenue
WHY NOT THE OTHERS?
A
If the firm’s equity investors hold a well-diversified portfolio, they would typically prefer that the firm hedge risks specific to the computer industry.
Diversified shareholders have already diversified away firm-specific risk.
C
Both equity and debt investors would typically prefer that the firm not hedge the foreign exchange risk of long-term contracts with international customers.
FX isn’t a core skill, and long contracts mean long exposure — hedge it.
D
Equity investors would typically not reward the firm for using hedging to reduce its tax exposure over a multi-year period.
Lower taxes mean higher after-tax earnings — shareholders like that.
KEY TAKEAWAY
1Lenders favor hedging: stable cash flows
2Diversified shareholders skip firm-specific hedges
3Hedging non-core risks & taxes adds value

Okay, pause the video and lock

And the answer is...
B!
Here's why.
Think about what a lender actually
gets.
Debt investors get their interest
and principal back,
and that's it.
They have little or no upside
when revenue jumps,
but they're the ones who get
hurt if revenue crashes.
So they'd love the firm to
hedge and keep its revenue stream
nice and stable.
Now, why not the others?
A is wrong.
Diversified equity investors have
already diversified away most firm-specific
risk in their own portfolios,
so they'd rather the firm didn't
spend money hedging it.
C is wrong too.
Foreign exchange isn't a core skill
for a computer maker,
and with long-term contracts,
the exposure lasts for years.
That's actually a strong case for
hedging.
And D?
Also wrong.
Hedging that reduces tax exposure
boosts after-tax earnings,
and equity investors would welcome
that.
So, the key takeaway.
Debt investors favor hedging because
they want stable cash flows.
Diversified shareholders don't
need firm-specific hedges.
And hedging non-core risks,
like foreign exchange,
or cutting taxes,
usually adds value.
