THE QUESTION

National regulators in an emerging market country are developing guidelines for appropriate risk management responsibilities for different business functions at the country’s banks. A regulatory analyst is asked to prepare a report recommending best practices for the firm-wide risk management, audit, and operations functions. Which of the following should the analyst recommend as an appropriate responsibility of the audit function?

Which is an appropriate responsibility of the audit function?

A✓✕

B✓✕
Implementing risk management policies related to stock-based compensation

D✓✕

CORRECT ANSWER
A✓✕
Analyzing correlation and volatility assumptions in a bank’s VaR models
Audit is the independent check
AUDIT’S ROLE
Independent review — not day-to-day running
SO AUDIT REVIEWS
Model assumptions
Volatility & correlation
VaR models
WHY NOT THE OTHERS?
B
Implementing risk management policies related to stock-based compensation
Senior management or the risk group sets these policies.
C
Monitoring risk exposures on a day-to-day basis for adherence to a bank’s concentration limits
Daily limit monitoring belongs to risk management.
D
Processing confirmations and settlements for trades executed by a bank
Confirmations and settlements belong to operations.
KEY TAKEAWAY
1Operations runs the trades
2Risk management monitors limits

And the answer is...
A!
Here's why.
Audit is the independent check.
It doesn't run the day-to-day business.
Instead, it reviews how things
are done, including whether the
models make sense.
So digging into the volatility
and correlation assumptions behind
a VaR model is exactly the
kind of review audit should do.
Now, why not the others?
Each one belongs to a different
team.
B is wrong.
Setting risk policies for stock-based
compensation is a job for senior
management or the risk management
group.
C is wrong too.
Daily monitoring of exposures against
concentration limits is the risk
management function's job.
And D?
That's
operations.
Processing confirmations and settlements
is back-office work.
So, the key takeaway.
Think of three lines of work.
Operations runs the trades.
Risk management monitors exposures
and limits.
And audit independently checks
it all, including the models.
