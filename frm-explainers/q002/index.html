PRACTICE QUESTION
THE QUESTION

A junior analyst at a bank is asked to provide suggestions on potential metrics the bank can use in its capital management program. The analyst prepares a presentation discussing the advantages and disadvantages of the RAROC metric. Which of the following statements is most appropriate for the analyst to include in the presentation?

Which statement about RAROC belongs in the presentation?
❚❚ Pause & pick your answer 321
A✓✕
RAROC will make it easier to compare the profitability of business divisions that require different levels of capital.
B✓✕
RAROC allows the firm to benchmark its performance against operating targets set by industry peers.
C✓✕
RAROC is an effective forward-looking tool to model potential extreme losses during stress scenarios.
D✓✕
An activity is adding value to the bank’s shareholders if its cost of equity capital is higher than its RAROC.
CORRECT ANSWER
A✓✕
RAROC will make it easier to compare the profitability of business divisions that require different levels of capital.
RAROC puts every business line on the same scale
THE FORMULA
Risk-adjusted return ÷ economic capital
SO YOU CAN COMPARE
A small desk
A big lending unit
Apples to apples
WHY NOT THE OTHERS?
B
RAROC allows the firm to benchmark its performance against operating targets set by industry peers.
Not a peer benchmark — it tests units against your own cost of equity.
C
RAROC is an effective forward-looking tool to model potential extreme losses during stress scenarios.
Built on expected returns — stress losses need scenario analysis.
D
An activity is adding value to the bank’s shareholders if its cost of equity capital is higher than its RAROC.
Backwards — value is added when RAROC beats the cost of equity.
KEY TAKEAWAY
1Compares units with different capital
2Adds value only if RAROC > cost of equity
3Not a peer benchmark or stress test

Here's why.
Different business lines need very
different amounts of economic capital,
and RAROC puts them all on
the same footing.
You take the risk-adjusted return
and divide it by the economic
capital it uses.
So a small desk and a

Now, why not the others?
B is wrong.
RAROC isn't a peer benchmark.
It tells you whether your own
business units earn more than your
cost of equity capital.
C is wrong too.
RAROC is built on expected returns.
It doesn't model extreme losses
in a stress scenario...
for that, you'd use tools like
scenario analysis.
And D has it backwards.
An activity adds value when its
RAROC is higher than the cost
of equity capital,
not lower.
So, the key takeaway.
RAROC compares business lines that
use different amounts of capital.
A business adds value only when
its RAROC beats its cost of
equity.
And it's not a peer benchmark
or a stress test.
